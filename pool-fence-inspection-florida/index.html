Pool Fence Inspection Guide

A homeowner's pre-check for pool barrier inspections in Hillsborough and Pasco counties.

Get a Free Estimate Call 813-669-4511

Lutz straddles the Hillsborough-Pasco line, so depending on your address, your inspector may come from either county. The state barrier rules are the same everywhere. Use this walk-through a few days before your inspection.

Before you start

This checklist follows Florida's pool barrier law (s. 515.29). Your inspector also applies the Florida Building Code and any local rules, which add exact measurements for openings and latch heights, so treat this as a pre-check, not a pass guarantee. When we build the barrier, we build to the code your inspector uses.

Common issues we see around Lutz

On lake lots, soil erosion under the fence after a rainy summer is the big one: a gap that wasn't there when the fence went in. On older homes, it's sliding glass doors that open straight onto the pool deck with no alarm. Both are easy fixes if you catch them before the inspector does.

1. Height, measured from the outside

The barrier must be at least 4 feet high measured on the outside, the side a child would approach from. Check low spots: a fence that's 48 inches on a level patio can come up short where the yard slopes or where mulch and soil have built up against it.

2. No way under, through or over

Look for gaps under the bottom rail, especially where rain has washed soil away, and for openings a small child could squeeze through. Check that horizontal rails or decorative pieces don't give a foothold on the outside of the fence.

3. The gate

Open the gate and let go. It should swing shut and latch on its own, every time, from any position. It must open outward, away from the pool, and the latch release needs to be on the pool side, out of a young child's reach. Sagging gates that drag or latches that don't catch are the most common reasons a barrier fails.

4. Climbing aids

Walk the outside of the fence. Pool equipment, AC units, planters, benches, trash cans and play sets placed near the barrier can count as a way over it. Move anything portable before inspection day and plan permanent equipment so it isn't next to the fence.

5. House doors and windows

If a wall of your house is part of the barrier, no door or window can open directly onto the pool unless it has one of the approved safety features, such as an exit alarm or a self-closing, self-latching door device (s. 515.27). This is the item most homeowners don't expect.

6. Distance from the water

The barrier has to sit far enough from the water's edge that a child or frail adult who gets past it can't fall straight into the pool. Fences built right at the coping can be a problem.

At a Glance

  • Based on Florida Statutes 515.27 and 515.29
  • A walk-through you can do yourself
  • The gate is the most common failure
  • Remove climbing aids before inspection day
  • Local codes can add requirements
